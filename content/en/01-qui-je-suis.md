# Who I am

## In a few words

My name is Maël Compagnon and I have loved computing since I was very young. Now in my 4th year at ESAIP, with an AI major, I am mostly interested in what happens when AI leaves the screen and acts in the real world: robotics, computer vision, machines that see and decide.

I hold one conviction: you can only design a system well if you have understood the people who work with it. That is why I spent a summer on my feet in the cold, on a salmon production line in Norway, before supervising a team of 22 people and then operating a robotic cell. And it is why, at weekends, I would rather do a hackathon than get 48 hours of sleep.

## Why the North?

I chose Finland for the academic level of SeAMK, in particular its industrial robotics courses, and Norway for the hands-on experience. In both cases there was also a less academic reason: I love nature, and both countries are rather well equipped on that front.

## Knowing myself

> Grid produced from course 3 "Knowing yourself". Each statement is tied to a situation I have lived.

### What comes fairly easily to me
- **Understanding how a system really works.** At SalMar, whenever there was a breakdown, I went to see the technicians and traced the machine's logic back to the program. That is what earned me my first promotion.
- **Adapting to an unfamiliar environment.** An isolated island, 52 nationalities, a language I did not speak: I settled in within a few weeks.
- **Explaining technical things simply.** Two years of computer classes for seniors taught me to make things accessible without talking down.
- **Moving a team project forward.** At Junction, in 48 hours, I carried the analysis part of our project, where the NVIDIA Nemotron model investigates each suspicious edit, while keeping up good energy in the team.

### What is harder for me
- **Delegating early.** At club ANT, the two of us carried everything for a long time before opening the project up to other volunteers. I learned to document and hand over, but it is a reflex I am still working on.
- **Stopping when it is "good enough".** I am a perfectionist. The hackathon taught me the value of a deadline: in 48 hours, you deliver what matters.
- **Acting without having understood everything.** I like to understand before acting. That is a strength when tracing a fault, but sometimes a brake when I should be prototyping fast.

### What I like
- The unknown and challenges that put me slightly out of my depth: a new country, a new technology, a first hackathon.
- Learning from the field and from the people who work there.
- Multicultural teams and flat organisations, where you can talk to the director as to a colleague.
- Useful technology that concretely serves people.

### What I like less
- Being a spectator. During my first internship, watching people code without being able to take part quickly felt frustrating: I asked for a project.
- Problems that people stop seeing. At SalMar, one defect had been costing a full-time position for months, and nobody questioned it any more.
- Overly rigid hierarchies, which slow down the flow of information.

## Me in a professional context

Three strengths, each backed by an example:

| Strength | The evidence |
|---|---|
| Technical curiosity | At SalMar, I suggested reprogramming a conveyor's controller to separate the fillets at the machine's exit. Nobody had asked me to, and the idea was tested over a full shift. |
| Adaptability | Erasmus in Finland, 15 weeks in Norway with 52 nationalities, then a first hackathon finished with a prize. |
| Sense of responsibility | Promoted to operator after 2 months (against 1 to 2 years on average on the site), leading 22 people. Co-president of club ANT for two years. |

Three points to watch, and what I do to improve:

| Point to watch | What I put in place |
|---|---|
| Perfectionism | I set a time limit for each task and define in advance what "done" means. |
| I delegate late | I document from the start, so the work can be taken over: this is the "toolbox" left to the team that took over club ANT. |
| I want to understand everything before acting | I practise prototyping fast, then understanding by iterating. Junction was a very good exercise for that. |

**What motivates me:** understanding how things work, seeing the concrete impact of my work, and learning from people who are not like me.

**A good manager, for me:** someone who trusts before demanding, who checks that an instruction has been understood rather than assuming it, and who stays approachable. My leader at SalMar moved me up to operator when I was only a young intern, then invited me to come back as assistant leader. That is the model I want to follow.

**What sets me apart:** I am a future AI engineer who started at the very bottom of the ladder. I know what people working alongside the machines we automate go through, and I do not forget that AI has to work on a real line, 24/7, not only in a notebook.
